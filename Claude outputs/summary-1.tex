\section*{\centering Reproducibility Summary}

\subsubsection*{Scope of Reproducibility}

Errica et al.~\cite{Errica:2025} measure how LLM classifiers react to
rephrased prompts with two metrics: \emph{sensitivity} (how much the
prediction for one sample changes across rephrasings) and \emph{consistency}
(how similarly the samples of one class are treated). We test three claims:
sensitivity (1), consistency (2) and micro-F1 (3) vary with the prompting
strategy and the dataset, and no single strategy gives the best micro-F1 on
every dataset. We reproduce the paper's open models, Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct
and Llama-3-70B-Instruct, and extend the study to Qwen3.5-27B and the small
reasoning model DeepSeek-R1 7B.

\subsubsection*{Methodology}

We ran the authors' code on the five datasets of the paper (TREC, CB, RTE,
DBPedia, WoS) with 30 prompt variants per sample, three prompting strategies,
temperature 0 and seed 42, after checking every data file against its official
source. Mixtral (FP16) and Llama-3-70B (FP8) ran with vLLM on one NVIDIA GB10
GPU (113.8 and 33.0 GPU hours), Qwen3.5-27B with vLLM on two L40S GPUs (32.2
GPU hours), and DeepSeek-R1 7B (4-bit) with Ollama on one GB10, on TREC and CB
only (24.5 GPU hours).

\subsubsection*{Results}

All three claims hold for all four models. The paper's numbers are only partly
reproduced. For Mixtral, the mean absolute error (MAE) is at most 0.015 on
WoS, DBPedia and TREC, but 0.053 on CB and 0.060 on RTE, and the best strategy
matches the paper in 12 of 15 cases. For Llama-3-70B, sensitivity and
consistency are close (MAE 0.008 and 0.005), micro-F1 less so (0.056), and the
best strategy matches in 10 of 15 cases. Qwen3.5-27B is less sensitive than
Mixtral on every dataset (sensitivity below 0.04 in 13 of 15 configurations).
On CB, DeepSeek-R1 7B has a higher micro-F1 than Mixtral but also a higher
sensitivity.

\subsubsection*{What was easy}

The authors released code, data and notebooks with their own outputs. New
models needed only an entry in the code's model table, and the stored results
let us recompute every metric ourselves.

\subsubsection*{What was difficult}

The paper does not state the serving setup or the weight precision. The paper
and the code disagree on the CB and RTE splits, the public TREC notebook was
truncated, and two values of the paper's Table~1 most likely have a decimal
error. Two of us had never used an HPC cluster, and long GPU queues slowed the
work.

\subsubsection*{Communication with original authors}

We contacted the first author. He told us that Mixtral most likely ran in FP16
and Llama-3-70B in INT8, without certainty, and he uploaded a complete version
of the TREC notebook.
